Value Added Tax (VAT) is one of the most complex and frequently mismanaged taxes for businesses in Kenya. Get the registration wrong — or delay it too long — and you face a combination of back-taxes, penalties, and the very real risk of losing business from clients who require VAT-registered suppliers.

In 2026, KRA has strengthened enforcement around VAT compliance, with particular focus on identifying businesses operating above the registration threshold without a VAT PIN. If your turnover is approaching or has exceeded Ksh 5 million, you need to act now.

What Is VAT in Kenya?

VAT is a consumption tax levied on the supply of taxable goods and services in Kenya at a standard rate of 16%. It is collected by VAT-registered businesses on behalf of KRA and remitted monthly. The VAT Act, 2013 (as amended) is the primary legislation governing VAT in Kenya, with the rate and exemptions periodically updated through the Finance Act.

The VAT system is designed as a flow-through tax: you charge your customers VAT (output tax), you pay VAT to your suppliers (input tax), and the difference is what you remit to KRA. If your input tax exceeds your output tax in any month, you have a VAT credit — which KRA can refund or carry forward.

Who Must Register for VAT in Kenya?

VAT registration in Kenya is mandatory for businesses that meet any of the following conditions:

  • Turnover threshold: Your annual taxable turnover reaches or is expected to reach Ksh 5 million. You must register within 30 days of reaching this threshold.
  • Taxable imports: You regularly import taxable goods into Kenya for commercial purposes.
  • Government and institutional suppliers: Some public sector and institutional buyers require their suppliers to be VAT-registered regardless of turnover — especially for tenders.
  • New businesses with anticipated turnover: If you reasonably expect to exceed Ksh 5 million in the next 12 months, you should register proactively.

Who Can Register Voluntarily?

Businesses with turnover below Ksh 5 million can apply for voluntary VAT registration. This is strategically useful if you purchase significant inputs that attract VAT — voluntary registration lets you recover that input tax, improving your cash position. How to Register for VAT in Kenya — The iTax Process (2026)

1

Log in to KRA iTax

Access your KRA iTax account at itax.kra.go.ke using your KRA PIN and password. Your business must already have a KRA PIN — if not, this must be done first.

2

Navigate to Registration

Go to Registration → PIN Certificate → Amendment. Select VAT as the tax obligation you wish to add to your PIN registration.

3

Complete the VAT Registration Form

Fill in your business details, nature of taxable supplies, bank account information, and the effective date of registration. This form determines your first VAT return period.

4

Upload Supporting Documents

Attach the required documentation (listed below). All documents must be current, clear, and in the correct format. Rejected documents are the most common cause of delays.

5

Await KRA Approval

KRA reviews the application and either approves it (typically 3–7 working days) or requests additional information. Once approved, your PIN is updated to reflect VAT registration.

6

Begin Monthly VAT Filing

From your effective registration date, you must file a VAT return by the 20th of every subsequent month — even if it is a nil return.

Documents Required for VAT Registration in Kenya

  • Certificate of Incorporation or Business Name Registration (from the Registrar of Companies)
  • Current KRA PIN Certificate for the business
  • National ID or passport of the director(s) / business owner
  • Current bank statement (last 3 months) showing the business account
  • Business premises lease agreement or ownership documents
  • Description of the nature of business and the goods/services supplied
  • Evidence of revenue (invoices, contracts, or bank credits) supporting the turnover claim

Common VAT Registration Mistakes in Kenya

  • Registering with the wrong effective date: Your effective date determines when you are liable for output VAT and eligible for input VAT claims. Setting it incorrectly can cost you recoverable input tax or expose you to back-liability.
  • Submitting outdated documents: KRA rejects bank statements older than 3 months or ID copies that are unclear. This delays registration and can trigger queries.
  • Not understanding VAT obligations post-registration: Many businesses register for VAT and then fail to file returns on time — immediately incurring penalties on a compliance obligation they just voluntarily took on.
  • Misclassifying exempt vs. zero-rated supplies: Exempt and zero-rated supplies are treated differently in VAT accounting. Misclassification leads to incorrect returns and potential KRA queries.
  • Missing the 30-day registration window: Once you exceed the Ksh 5 million threshold, you have 30 days to register. Businesses that miss this window face back-dated VAT liability from the date of threshold breach.

Penalties for Late or Non-Registration in Kenya

  • Late registration penalty: Ksh 10,000 fixed penalty for failing to register within the 30-day window after reaching the threshold.
  • Back-tax liability: KRA can assess VAT on all taxable supplies made since the date your turnover exceeded Ksh 5 million — not just from when you registered.
  • Interest: 1% per month on all unpaid VAT, compounded from the date each return was due.
  • Loss of business: Corporate clients and government bodies increasingly require VAT-registered suppliers. Operating unregistered can cost you tenders and long-term contracts.
Key Takeaways

  • KRA can back-date VAT liability to the date of threshold breach — penalties and interest can be substantial if delayed.
