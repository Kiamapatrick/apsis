Where Do You Serve a KRA Objection or Customs Review? What Businesses Should Know

When a business disagrees with a KRA decision, the first question is usually whether it has a case. A second question gets far less attention: where does the paperwork actually go?

On 23 November 2026, KRA published two public notices that answer that question for two types of dispute. Here is how we read them, and what we would want any business to check before serving a document.

At a Glance

Domestic tax objections Customs review applications
Covers persons aggrieved by a decision of The Commissioner, Large Taxpayers and/or the Commissioner, Micro & Small Taxpayers The Commissioner, Customs & Border Control
Legal provision cited Section 73, Tax Procedures Act (Cap. 469B) Section 229, East African Community Customs Management Act, 2004
What is served Notices of objection and related documents Applications for review and related documents
Effective From the date of publication of the notice From the date of publication of the notice

The two notices are separate and cite different laws, so they should not be treated as interchangeable.

Which Notice Applies to You?

If the decision came from a domestic tax office named above, the first notice is the relevant one. It deals with notices of objection under Section 73 of the Tax Procedures Act.

If the decision came from Customs & Border Control, the second notice is the relevant one. It deals with applications for review under Section 229 of the EACCMA, and also references Gazette Notice No. 1036 of 2019 on the delegation of powers to the Commissioner, Legal & Board Services Department.

Where Do the Documents Go?

Both notices designate the same two channels.

Designated Channels

Email: LSBCIROStaff@kra.go.ke

Physical delivery or post:

The Deputy Commissioner,
Tax Dispute Resolution Division,
Legal & Board Services Department,
Block B, 7th Floor, Ushuru Pension Towers (UPT),
Elgon Road, Upper Hill,
P.O. Box 48240 – 00100, Nairobi.

KRA also directs any clarification to the same email address.

How We Would Approach It

Before serving any document, we would run through these steps:

  1. Pin down the decision. Which Commissioner issued it, and under which law? That tells you which notice applies.
  2. Match it to the right notice. Domestic objection or customs review. Don’t assume the process is the same.
  3. Serve through the designated channel. Use the email or postal address named in the applicable notice.
  4. Keep proof. Save a copy of everything you submit, the sent email, and any delivery or postage confirmation.
  5. Read the decision letter. The notices don’t say whether other channels remain available. If your letter gives different instructions, clarify before you send anything.

Why We Pay Attention to This

In our view, a tax dispute is not only about who has the better argument. How and where documents are served is part of the process, and it is the part most easily checked in advance. These notices are a useful prompt for businesses to confirm their own process before an objection or review application goes out.

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Who issued these notices?

The Commissioner for Legal and Board Services at KRA, on 23 November 2026.

Do the notices apply to every tax decision?

No. The domestic notice names decisions of the Commissioner, Large Taxpayers and/or the Commissioner, Micro & Small Taxpayers. The customs notice names decisions of the Commissioner, Customs & Border Control. Check which one your decision falls under.

When do they take effect?

From the date of publication of each notice.

What email address do the notices give?

LSBCIROStaff@kra.go.ke, for both serving documents and seeking clarification.
