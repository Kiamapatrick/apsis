Pending Government Bills and Tax Payment: KRA Enforcement Relaxation Guidelines

The Kenya Revenue Authority has published taxpayer guidelines that allow for relaxation of enforcement measures in verified cases where outstanding tax liabilities arise from pending bills owed by national government ministries, departments and agencies (MDAs), county governments, and state corporations. This article summarises the guideline in plain language so affected taxpayers can understand the conditions, the process, and what relief may involve.

At a Glance

  • Covers tax arrears directly attributable to pending bills from national/county government contracts or supplies.
  • Applies to enforcement actions for Large and Medium Taxpayers (LMT) and Micro and Small Taxpayers (MST).
  • Relaxation is granted only where five conditions are satisfied (formal application, documentary proof, verification, return filing compliance, agency notice).
  • Relief may include temporary non-issuance of Tax Compliance Certificates (TCC) and/or distress for the verified debt.
  • The Commissioner notifies the decision within 30 days; extensions are reviewed every three months.
  • Late payment interest continues to accrue from the original tax due date even if an extension is granted.

Who It Applies To

The guideline applies to enforcement actions by:

  • Large and Medium Taxpayers (LMT) and Micro and Small Taxpayers (MST).
  • Taxpayers whose outstanding taxes are directly attributable to pending national or county government contracts or supplies.

The pending bills must be owed by national government MDAs, county governments, or state corporations.

The Five Conditions for Relaxation

Relaxation is granted only where all of the following conditions are satisfied:

a) Formal Application

The taxpayer must submit a written application to the Head of Debt at the Tax Service Office (TSO) requesting an extension of time to pay the tax due. The application must specify:

  • The tax period, tax type, and amount outstanding.
  • Invoice-level reconciliation showing the link to the pending government contracts and private sector receivables.

b) Documentary Proof

Supporting documents may include:

  • A letter confirming pending bills from the authorised accounting officer or Head of Finance of the relevant MDA or county.
  • Copies of valid contracts, delivery notes, invoices, or payment certificates.
  • Evidence of effort to recover the pending bills.
  • Bank statements showing delayed receivables.
  • Audited financial statements.
  • VAT invoice-level reconciliation schedules where applicable.

Not accepted: unverified letters, undocumented claims, supplier disputes, or general assertions of hardship. The source uses both "or" and "and" in this list; these documents may include the items above and no single item is stated as compulsory.

c) Verification

Verification is done using official MDA or county communication channels, designated customer contact persons, authorised signatories, and standardised verification templates. KRA states it will maintain a centralised, continuously updated database of MDA and county contacts to support verification.

d) Return Filing Compliance

The taxpayer should have filed all relevant tax returns relating to the cash flow constraints, including pending bills, on time as required by law, even if unable to pay on time.

e) Agency Notice

The taxpayer countersigns the Agency Notice addressed to the Accounting Officer or Head of Finance of the national or county government entity (or private entity) that owes the pending bill.

Documents That May Be Requested

In addition to the items listed under condition (b), KRA may request further supporting documentation during verification. Taxpayers should be prepared to provide:

  • Official confirmation of pending bills from the relevant government entity's authorised accounting officer or Head of Finance.
  • Valid contracts, delivery notes, invoices, or payment certificates.
  • Records demonstrating active efforts to recover the pending bills.
  • Bank statements evidencing delayed receivables.
  • Audited financial statements.
  • VAT invoice-level reconciliation schedules where applicable.

Unverified letters, undocumented claims, supplier disputes, and general assertions of hardship are not accepted.

What Relief May Look Like

After verification and approval, the following measures may be considered (the guideline states "including but not limited to"):

  • Temporary relaxation of enforcement actions for the specific verified debt. This may include non-issuance of a Tax Compliance Certificate (TCC) and/or distress.
  • The extension is valid for a defined period decided by the Commissioner, subject to review. No fixed length of time is stated.
  • The Commissioner notifies the taxpayer in writing of the decision within 30 days of receiving the application for extension of time.
  • Upon relaxation, the taxpayer countersigns an Agency Notice addressed to the Accounting Officer of the government entity or private entity that owes the pending bill, invoice, or contract.

Decision Timeline, Review and Revocation

  • Decision: The Commissioner notifies the taxpayer in writing within 30 days of receiving the application.
  • Review: Every three months, or earlier if circumstances change.
  • Revocation triggers:
    • Verification fails or the pending bill, invoice, or contract remains unsettled after the agreed extension period.
    • Default on the arrangement.
    • Emergence of new unarranged debt.
    • Misrepresentation.
    • The Commissioner may revoke any extension and reinstate enforcement at any time it comes to notice that the taxpayer violated the conditions.
  • A Tax Compliance Certificate is revoked upon default, emergence of new unarranged debt, or misrepresentation.
  • KRA will not engage directly with suppliers or intervene in commercial disputes; its relationship is solely with the taxpayer.
  • The taxpayer must notify the Commissioner immediately if payment is made without deducting the tax due as provided under the countersigned agency notice.

Interest Still Applies

Even if granted an extension of time to pay, or permission to pay by instalments, the taxpayer is liable for late payment interest from the original date the tax was due. The guideline does not waive interest.

What This Guideline Does Not Do

  • Does not guarantee or automate relief — relaxation is subject to verification and approval.
  • Does not waive penalties or interest.
  • Does not engage KRA directly with suppliers or intervene in commercial disputes.
  • Does not specify which tax types are covered (the guideline applies to taxes arising from pending bills as described).
  • Does not state a fixed extension period — the Commissioner decides the defined period.
  • Does not provide instalment arrangements as a standalone relief (instalments are mentioned only in the context of interest liability).

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Who can apply for enforcement relaxation under this guideline?

Large and Medium Taxpayers (LMT) and Micro and Small Taxpayers (MST) whose outstanding taxes are directly attributable to pending bills from national or county government contracts or supplies (including MDAs and state corporations).

What documents must I submit with my application?

A written application to the Head of Debt at your Tax Service Office specifying the tax period, type, and amount, plus invoice-level reconciliation. Supporting documents may include a letter from the MDA/county accounting officer or Head of Finance, contracts, delivery notes, invoices, payment certificates, evidence of recovery efforts, bank statements, audited financial statements, and VAT reconciliation schedules. No single document is stated as compulsory.

How long does KRA take to decide?

The Commissioner notifies the taxpayer in writing of the decision within 30 days of receiving the application for extension of time.

Is interest waived if I get an extension?

No. Even with an extension of time to pay or permission to pay by instalments, late payment interest applies from the original date the tax was due.

Can KRA revoke the relaxation after granting it?

Yes. The Commissioner may revoke any extension and reinstate enforcement if verification fails, the pending bill remains unsettled after the agreed period, the taxpayer defaults, new unarranged debt emerges, or misrepresentation is discovered. Reviews occur every three months or earlier if circumstances change.
